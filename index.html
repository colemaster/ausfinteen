What Will I Learn Preview

1. What Career Options Are Available To Me?

As a teen, popular first jobs include casual retail (supermarkets, fashion), fast food, cafes/restaurants, tutoring, swimming teaching, and sports coaching. You can also explore apprenticeships and traineeships!

2. How Do Study Loans (HECS-HELP & VET) Work?

HECS-HELP loans cover your university tuition fees upfront. You don't repay a single cent until your income reaches the compulsory repayment threshold ($67,000 in 2026-27, at a 1% rate under Australia's marginal HELP repayment system!).

3. How Do I Put Together An A-Class Resume?

Keep it 1 page! Include your contact details, education/school year, key skills (punctual, friendly, teamwork), any volunteering/school roles, and availability (e.g. weekends, weeknights).

4. How Do I Write An A-Class Cover Letter?

Address the manager by name if possible. Explain why you love their store/brand, highlight 2 key strengths with quick examples, and state your enthusiasm to learn and work hard.

5. How Do I Prep For & Ace Job Interview Questions?

Practice the STAR method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for questions like "Tell me about a time you handled a tricky problem." Dress neatly, make eye contact, smile, and bring energy!

6. What Do Junior Award Pay Rates Mean?

Under Modern Awards (Retail, Fast Food, Hospitality), under-18 workers are paid a legally fixed percentage of the adult award rate: 15yo = 40%, 16yo = 50%, 17yo = 60%, 18yo = 70%. With the 2026-27 adult Level 1 rate of $27.81/hr (4.75% rise from 1 July 2026), a 15-year-old earns about $11.12/hr before the 25% casual loading. Big 2026 change: from 1 December 2026, 18-20 year olds in Retail, Fast Food and Pharmacy move to the FULL adult rate after 6 months with the same employer (FWC [2026] FWCFB 75). Under-18 rates are unchanged.

7. What To Look For Before Signing Your Job Contract?

Check: 1) Job type (Casual, Part-Time, Full-Time), 2) Hourly pay rate, 3) Superannuation details, 4) Penalty rates for weekends/nights, and 5) Ensure you get a Fair Work Information Statement.

8. What Admin Obligations Do I Have Starting A New Job?

You must provide your employer with: 1) Your Tax File Number (TFN Declaration form), 2) Bank account details (BSB & Account Number), and 3) Superannuation Choice Form.

9. How Do I Interpret My Payslip?

Your payslip must show: Gross Pay (earnings before tax), PAYG Tax Withheld, Net Pay (money deposited in bank), Super contribution amount, Hourly rate, Hours worked, and Allowances.

10. How Do I Fill Out ATO TFN Declaration Form NAT 3092?

When starting a job, complete ATO Form NAT 3092. Enter your TFN, personal details, and MUST select "YES" to Question 8 ("Do you want to claim the tax-free threshold from this payer?") so your employer doesn't withhold unnecessary tax!

11. How Do I Fill Out ATO Super Choice Form NAT 13080?

Complete ATO Form NAT 13080 to instruct your employer to pay super into your existing stapled fund. You need: Fund Name, Unique Superannuation Identifier (USI), and your Member Account Number.

12. What Are Mandatory Fair Work FWIS & CEIS Statements?

By law under the Fair Work Act, employers MUST give all new workers the Fair Work Information Statement (FWIS), and casual workers the Casual Employment Information Statement (CEIS). They explain your legal minimum rights.

13. What Are Legal Unpaid Trial Shift Rules in Australia?

An unpaid trial is ONLY legal if it is a brief skills evaluation (typically 1 to 2 hours maximum) to demonstrate basic tasks. ANY work performed beyond a brief trial MUST be paid at your full legal award rate!

14. Why Cash-In-Hand Is A Dangerous Scam for Young Workers

Working "cash-in-hand" off the books strips away your legal protections: 1) You lose WorkCover injury insurance, 2) You get $0 super paid, 3) Employers underpay you below minimum wage, and 4) You face ATO penalties.

15. How Do Weekend & Public Holiday Penalty Rates Work?

Under Modern Awards, working weekends or public holidays earns higher penalty pay: Saturday = +25% to +50%, Sunday = +50% to +100%, Public Holidays = +125% to +150% (Double time and a half)!

16. Barefoot Investor Advice: The "Dominoes" First Job Strategy

Scott Pape (*The Barefoot Investor*) highlights that your first part-time job (delivering pizzas, stacking shelves) is about building work ethic, punctuality, and a glowing reference—one of the core Barefoot milestones!

17. Barefoot Investor Advice: Setting Up Your 3 Paycheck Buckets

Pape recommends splitting your teen paycheck into 3 buckets: 1) Blow Bucket (60% for daily fun & food), 2) Mojo Bucket (20% for a $2,000 emergency buffer), and 3) Grow Bucket (20% for a car bought in CASH).

18. Broke Millennial Advice: How To Ask Your Boss For A Pay Raise

Erin Lowry (*Broke Millennial*) advises asking for a pay raise after 6-12 months based on your documented achievements and added responsibilities—never on personal financial need.

19. Broke Millennial Advice: Politely Addressing Payslip Underpayment

If your payslip is missing hours or penalty rates, approach your manager assuming a simple accounting error: "Hi Manager, I noticed my payslip logged X hours instead of Y. Could we review the log together?"

20. What Are Your Workplace Health & Safety (WHS) Rights?

Under SafeWork Australia regulations, employers must provide proper safety induction, training, and PPE. You have a legal right to refuse unsafe work without fear of losing your job!

21. How Old Do I Need To Be To Get A Job In Australia?

At 15 you can work in most casual jobs in Queensland (retail, fast food, cafes, tutoring, umpiring). Under 13s can only do light work like paper rounds with a parent's permission, and some states have extra permits for younger teens. No matter your age, you can NEVER be rostered during school hours — school attendance comes first, and employers must follow state child employment rules. Some jobs are restricted by age: you must be 18 to serve alcohol or work in venues like bars and TABs.

22. How Many Hours Can I Work While At School?

Queensland law doesn't cap weekly hours for 15-year-olds, but school must always come first — employers cannot roster you during school hours, and you must not work so much that your attendance or results suffer. As a guide, most schools and parents agree 8-12 hours a week during term (e.g. 2 weekend shifts) is plenty, and many teens ramp up to 20+ hours in the holidays. Remember: under 18 you only receive super when you work MORE than 30 hours in a week.

23. What Is A School-Based Apprenticeship Or Traineeship (SAT)?

From Year 10 you can start a School-Based Apprenticeship or Traineeship (SAT) in QLD: you are employed part-time by a real business (e.g. hairdressing, retail, IT, hospitality), attend TAFE one day a week, and keep studying at school. You earn a wage, get paid super, work towards a national qualification, and it can count towards your QCE — a massive head start on your career before you finish school.

24. Where Do 15-Year-Olds Actually Get Hired In Australia?

Top Australian youth employers hiring 15-year-olds: 1) Supermarkets (Woolworths & Coles apply online via career portals), 2) Fast Food (McDonald’s via Olivia AI, Hungry Jack’s, KFC, Guzman y Gomez, Boost Juice), 3) Retail (Kmart, Target, Big W), and 4) Local sports refereeing (Soccer, Basketball, Netball — pays $20–$35/game in cash or bank transfer!). For local bakeries and cafes, walking in between 2pm–4pm on weekdays with a printed resume works best.

25. What Is The 3-Hour Minimum Shift Rule?

Under Modern Awards (Fast Food Industry Award MA000003 & General Retail Industry Award MA000004), casual shifts must be a minimum of 3 consecutive hours. If a manager sends you home after 1.5 hours because the store is quiet, by law they MUST still pay you for the full 3 hours!

26. What Meal & Rest Breaks Am I Legally Entitled To?

Under Fair Work Modern Awards: 1) Shifts 4 to 5 hours = 1 × 10-minute rest break (100% PAID by employer). 2) Shifts 5 to 7 hours = 1 × 10-minute paid rest break + 1 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal break. 3) Shifts 7+ hours = 2 × 10-minute paid rest breaks + 1 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal break. You can never be forced to work 6+ hours without a food break!

27. First Shift Survival Guide: What Should I Bring & Expect?

On your very first shift: 1) Wear enclosed, comfortable, black non-slip shoes and neat black pants/skirt. 2) Arrive 10 minutes early. 3) Bring your TFN, bank BSB & account number, emergency contact details, and a water bottle. 4) Don’t be afraid to ask questions — managers expect you to learn on the job!

28. Why Sports Refereeing Is The Best Kept Secret For 15yo Income

Refereeing junior sports (soccer, basketball, netball, touch footy) is one of the highest-paying first jobs for 15-year-olds! Junior referees earn $20 to $35 per game. Ref 3 games on a Saturday morning and you can pocket $75–$100 in just 3 hours while staying fit and building leadership skills.
