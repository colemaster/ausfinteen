💰Tax & Tax Returns

13. $1,000 Standard Deduction (From 2026-27): No Receipts Needed

From 1 July 2026, workers with wage income automatically get up to a $1,000 deduction against labour income — no receipts, no records, no spending required (worth ~$160–$450 depending on your bracket). It is capped at your labour income and reduces taxable income, not your tax bill directly. Claim actual expenses instead only if they exceed $1,000 (then you need full records). Union and professional fees stay claimable ON TOP. First claimed in the 2026–27 return you lodge in July 2027.

1. What Are Modern Awards & How Do Junior Pay Rates Work?

Modern Awards are legally binding industry standards set and indexed annually by the Fair Work Commission (FWC). Junior pay is set as a statutory percentage of the adult Level 1 base rate ($27.81/hr for 2026-27 under Fast Food [MA000003] and General Retail [MA000004]; $26.44/hr under Restaurant [MA000119] and Hospitality [MA000009]). Under the Fast Food Award, workers under 16 receive 40% ($11.12/hr base, or $13.90/hr with 25% casual loading). Under General Retail and Community Pharmacy [MA000012], workers under 16 receive 45% ($12.51/hr base, or $15.64/hr casual). Junior percentages scale by age: 16yo = 50% ($13.91/hr base / $17.38–$17.39/hr casual), 17yo = 60% ($16.69/hr base / $20.86/hr casual), 18yo = 70% ($19.47/hr base / $24.33–$24.34/hr casual), 19yo = 80% ($22.25/hr base / $27.81/hr casual), and 20yo = 90% ($25.03/hr base / $31.29/hr casual) in Fast Food, whereas in General Retail 20-year-olds earn 100% full adult rate ($27.81/hr base / $34.76/hr casual) after 6 months of employment (and in Restaurant and Hospitality, 20-year-olds receive the 100% full adult rate from day one!). Casual loading (+25%) is calculated on your junior base rate. Note: Minor 1-cent differences ($17.38 vs $17.39, $24.33 vs $24.34) depend on whether payroll systems apply the 25% loading to the exact unrounded or rounded base; both are compliant under Fair Work guidelines. Paying below these rates is unlawful wage theft under federal law.

2. What Is The 3-Hour Minimum Shift Rule Under Modern Awards?

Under major Australian awards including Fast Food [MA000003], General Retail [MA000004], Community Pharmacy [MA000012], Fitness [MA000094], and Amusement [MA000080], casual employees are legally entitled to a minimum of 3 hours pay per shift. In Restaurant [MA000119] and Hospitality [MA000009], the minimum is 2 hours (expanding to 4 hours on public holidays under Restaurant). Key distinction: Under the Fast Food Award (Clauses 10.2 & 11.3), the 3-hour minimum engagement is strict and mandatory for both part-time and casual employees with NO student exception. In General Retail, Clause 11.3 (and 15.7) creates a narrow exception permitting a 1.5-hour minimum shift strictly for full-time secondary school students working 3:00pm–6:30pm on school days with written parental consent. If your manager sends you home after 45 minutes because the shop or drive-thru is quiet, they are legally required to pay you for the full minimum shift duration (e.g. 3 full hours)!

3. What Are My Legal Rest Pause & Meal Break Entitlements?

Modern Awards mandate strict break schedules based on continuous shift length: 1) Shifts 4 to 5 hours: 1 × 10-minute rest break (100% PAID by the employer). 2) Shifts 5 to 7 hours: 1 × 10-minute paid rest break PLUS 1 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal break. 3) Shifts 7 to 10 hours: 2 × 10-minute paid rest breaks PLUS 1 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal break. 4) Shifts 10+ hours: 2 × 10-minute paid rest breaks PLUS 2 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal breaks. Employers cannot force you to work through paid rest pauses, combine them with unpaid meal breaks, or deduct pay for 10-minute breaks. You are on the clock and being paid while resting.

4. What Are The State Working Hours & Curfew Rules For 15-Year-Olds?

Every Australian state enforces statutory limits to safeguard compulsory education: In QLD (*Child Employment Act 2006*), 15yos who haven't completed Year 10 can work max 12 hrs/week in term, max 4 hrs on a school day, max 8 hrs on non-school days, max 38 hrs in holidays, with a strict 10:00pm–6:00am curfew and a mandatory signed Parent Consent Form CE1 (the chief-executive approved form). In VIC (*Child Employment Act 2003*), teens 15+ can work in retail/hospitality without an employer licence outside school hours (under-15s require an employer licence, max 3 hrs/school day, max 12 hrs/term week, and a 9:00pm curfew). In NSW, under-15s cannot work past 9:00pm on school nights, with a max 4 hrs/school day and a 50-hour combined school+work weekly limit. In WA, 15yos work under standard awards outside school hours (13–14yos require the Wageline Parent Permission Form). In SA, TAS, ACT, and NT, employment must not interfere with compulsory schooling.

5. How Do I Complete ATO Form NAT 3092 (TFN Declaration)?

When starting any new job, your employer must provide ATO Tax File Number Declaration Form (NAT 3092). Critical steps: 1) Fill in your legal name, date of birth, address, and 9-digit TFN. 2) At Question 8 ("Do you want to claim the tax-free threshold from this payer?"), tick YES if this is your primary or only job. The statutory tax-free threshold is $18,200/year. When combined with the $700 Low Income Tax Offset (LITO), you pay $0 income tax on annual earnings up to $22,575. Ticking YES stops your boss from withholding 16% to 30% PAYG tax from your pay. If you fail to provide a TFN within 28 days, your employer must legally withhold the top marginal tax rate of 47% from every dollar!

6. Are Unpaid Work Trials Legal In Australia?

Under Fair Work Ombudsman guidelines, unpaid trials are only lawful if they are brief skills evaluations strictly necessary to demonstrate relevant capabilities (e.g. demonstrating how to make 1–2 coffees or operate a till for up to 1 hour under direct supervision). Any trial that involves general shift labor, lasts multiple hours, or occurs during commercial service MUST be paid at your full award hourly rate plus casual loading. Working cash-in-hand off the books is strictly illegal: it strips away statutory WorkCover injury insurance, yields $0 superannuation, and constitutes criminal wage theft under federal laws enacted on 1 January 2025. Furthermore, employers who force teens to register an Australian Business Number (ABN) to treat them as independent contractors instead of employees commit illegal sham contracting under Section 357 of the Fair Work Act, exposing them to massive civil penalties.

7. What Is The 100-Point ID Checklist For 15-Year-Olds?

To open bank accounts, obtain a TFN via Australia Post, or pass employer HR onboarding, 15-year-olds need 100 points of identification: 1) Primary ID (70 pts): Australian Birth Certificate, Australian Passport, or Australian Citizenship Certificate. 2) Secondary Photo ID (40 pts): Current High School Student ID Card (with photo and legal name), State Proof of Age Card, or Learner Driver Licence. 3) Supporting ID (25 pts each): Green Medicare Card (as a dependant or own card), Bank Debit Card, Youth Transport Concession Card, or an official signed letter from your high school principal. Combining your Birth Certificate (70 pts), Student ID (40 pts), and Medicare Card (25 pts) totals 135 points, easily exceeding the 100-point threshold.

8. How Do I Fill Out ATO Super Choice Form NAT 13080 & Super Stapling?

Under the Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992, employers must contribute 12.0% Superannuation Guarantee (SG) into your nominated fund. For under-18 workers, super is legally compulsory if you work MORE than 30 hours in a given week (e.g. during school holidays or full-time traineeships); once you turn 18, super is paid on every dollar regardless of hours! Complete ATO Form NAT 13080 (Superannuation Standard Choice Form) by providing your Fund Name, Unique Superannuation Identifier (USI), and Member Number. Under Super Stapling legislation, the ATO links your super account to your TFN so it follows you between employers, preventing duplicate accounts from charging multiple monthly administration fees. Low balances under $6,000 are protected by a statutory 3% fee cap.

9. What Are The 12 Mandatory Items On A Legal Australian Payslip?

Under Section 536 of the Fair Work Act 2009 and Regulation 3.46 of the Fair Work Regulations 2009, employers must provide a written payslip within 1 working day of payday. Mandatory details include: 1) Employer legal name & ABN, 2) Employee legal name, 3) Pay period start and end dates, 4) Date of payment, 5) Gross pay (before tax), 6) Net pay (take-home cash transferred to your bank), 7) Ordinary hourly base rate, 8) Number of hours worked at that rate, 9) Itemised penalty rate multipliers (Saturday 125%/150%, Sunday 150%/175%, Public Holiday 250%), 10) Casual loading (+25%) clearly identified, 11) Itemised deductions (PAYG tax withheld), and 12) Superannuation contribution amount and super fund name (with Payday Super compliance showing contributions paid concurrently with wages). Failing to provide compliant payslips attracts civil penalties up to $93,900 for corporations.

10. Young Worker Safety, Workplace Health & WorkCover Rights

Every young Australian worker is protected under Safe Work Australia standards and statutory Work Health and Safety (WHS) legislation. Employers have a non-delegable duty of care to provide safe working conditions, personal protective equipment (PPE like non-slip shoes, aprons, and eye protection), and thorough supervised training on commercial machinery (deep fryers, meat slicers, compactors, and chemical cleaning products). Under Section 84 of the WHS Act, you have the absolute legal right to cease or refuse unsafe work without fear of disciplinary action or dismissal. Young workers are also protected against workplace bullying and sexual harassment under Fair Work Act s 789FD and employer positive duty under the Sex Discrimination Act. If injured on shift: 1) Report it immediately to your shift supervisor, 2) Visit a GP and request a WorkCover Certificate of Capacity, 3) Lodge a claim form with your employer and state insurer (WorkCover QLD, icare NSW, WorkSafe VIC). Statutory WorkCover covers 100% of medical bills and pays wage replacement for missed shifts.

11. Workplace Scripts: How To Hand In Resumes & Call In Sick Professionally

Professional workplace communication builds reputation and earns respect: 1) Handing in a resume in person: Visit cafes and shops during quiet mid-afternoon hours (2:00pm–4:00pm Tuesday–Thursday; never during the morning or lunch rush!). Dress neatly in smart-casual attire, smile, ask politely for the hiring manager, and deliver a 30-second pitch stating your weekend availability and enthusiasm. 2) Calling in sick: Call your manager directly at least 2 to 4 hours before your shift starts. State clearly that you are unwell and unfit to work, state when you anticipate returning, and offer to provide a pharmacist medical certificate or statutory declaration. Never text 10 minutes before shift start or ghost your manager.

12. School-Based Apprenticeships & Traineeships (SATs)

School-Based Apprenticeships and Traineeships (SATs) allow high school students in Years 10, 11, and 12 to combine paid work (typically 1 day/week earning award trainee rates of $15–$22/hr), structured vocational training at TAFE or an RTO, and regular high school studies. SATs count directly toward your senior secondary certificate (such as the Queensland Certificate of Education — QCE, HSC in NSW, or VCE in VIC), letting you graduate Year 12 with a nationally recognised qualification (Certificate II, III, or IV) and zero student debt. Popular industries include hospitality, business, retail, IT, early childhood education, automotive, electrical, and construction trades.

13. Cleaning Award Secret: Full Adult Pay From Day One (No Junior Rates!)

The Cleaning Services Award 2020 [MA000022] contains NO junior percentage rates for general cleaning staff — a 15-year-old cleaner earns 100% of the full adult Level 1 rate of $27.81/hr ($34.76/hr casual with 25% loading) from 1 July 2026. Penalty rates are exceptionally lucrative: Saturday casual is 150% ($41.72/hr), Sunday casual is 200% ($55.62/hr), and public holidays pay 250% ($69.53/hr casual)! That can double what retail and fast food juniors earn at the exact same age. Important nuance: Shopping trolley collection contracted out under retail or specialized contractor schedules may carry junior rates (50% under 16 = $13.91/hr base); always check your contract to confirm your legal award classification.

14. Retail vs Fast Food: Which Pays More on Sundays & Public Holidays?

Both the General Retail Award [MA000004] and Fast Food Award [MA000003] share the same adult Level 1 base rate of $27.81/hr ($34.76/hr casual) from 1 July 2026, but their weekend penalty multipliers differ substantially. In General Retail, casuals earn Sunday 175% ($48.67/hr adult, or $21.89/hr for a 15yo casual). In Fast Food, Level 1 casuals earn Sunday 150% ($41.72/hr adult, or $16.68/hr for a 15yo casual). On Public Holidays, both awards pay 250% casual ($69.53/hr adult, or $31.28/hr for a 15yo retail casual vs $27.81/hr for a 15yo fast food casual). A teenager working consistent Sunday shifts in a supermarket or retail store can earn $5.21/hr to $6.95/hr more than an equivalent fast food burger-flipper — always compare rosters and award penalty multipliers, not just base rates!

15. Modern Award Coverage Guide: Fast Food, Retail, Restaurant, Hospitality, Pharmacy & Fitness

Understanding which Modern Award governs your workplace ensures you receive the correct base pay, allowances, and minimum shift lengths: 1) Fast Food Industry Award 2020 [MA000003]: Covers McDonald’s, KFC, Hungry Jack’s, Domino’s, Subway. Level 1 adult base is $27.81/hr ($34.76/hr casual); 15yo junior rate is 40% ($11.12/hr base, $13.90/hr casual); 3-hour minimum casual shift. 2) General Retail Industry Award 2020 [MA000004]: Covers Coles, Woolworths, Kmart, Bunnings, clothing boutiques, and specialty shops. Level 1 adult base is $27.81/hr ($34.76/hr casual); 15yo junior rate is 45% ($12.51/hr base, $15.64/hr casual); 3-hour minimum shift (1.5-hr student exception applies). 3) Restaurant Industry Award 2020 [MA000119]: Covers dining cafes, bistros, and restaurants. Level 1 base is $26.44/hr ($33.05/hr casual); Under-17 rate is 50% ($13.22/hr base, $16.53/hr casual); 2-hour minimum casual shift (4 hours on public holidays). 4) Hospitality Industry (General) Award 2020 [MA000009]: Covers hotels, motels, resorts, function centres, and catering. Level 1 base is $26.44/hr ($33.05/hr casual); Under-17 rate is 50% ($13.22/hr base, $16.53/hr casual); 2-hour minimum shift. 5) Community Pharmacy Award 2020 [MA000012]: Covers Chemist Warehouse, TerryWhite Chemmart, Priceline. Level 1 base is $27.81/hr ($34.76/hr casual); 15yo junior rate is 45% ($12.51/hr base, $15.64/hr casual); 3-hour minimum shift. 6) Fitness Industry Award 2020 [MA000094]: Covers commercial gyms, council aquatic centres, swim schools, and personal training studios. Level 1 base is $27.81/hr ($34.76/hr casual); Under-17 rate is 55% ($15.30/hr base, $19.13/hr casual); 3-hour minimum shift. Note: Qualified fitness instructors (Cert III/IV Level 3+) receive 100% full adult rates regardless of age!

16. Weekend & Public Holiday Penalty Rates: How The 225%, 250% & 275% Multipliers Work

Penalty rates compensate employees for working outside standard Monday-to-Friday business hours. In Modern Awards, penalties are expressed as a percentage multiplier of the base hourly rate. For casuals, the 25% casual loading is generally integrated into the penalty multiplier rather than compounding: 1) Saturday Shift: Permanent staff receive 125% (time and a quarter). Casuals receive 150% (125% base + 25% casual loading) across Fast Food, Retail, Restaurant, Hospitality, and Pharmacy. 2) Sunday Shift: Permanent staff receive 150% (time and a half). Casuals receive 175% in General Retail and Hospitality; 150% in Fast Food Level 1 and Restaurant (Intro–Level 2); and up to 200% permanent / 225% casual in Community Pharmacy for evening trading outside 7:00am–9:00pm. 3) Public Holidays: Permanent staff receive 225% to 250% of base rate. Casuals receive 250% (double time and a half inclusive of casual loading) across Retail, Fast Food, Hospitality, Restaurant, and Pharmacy. In awards with 250% permanent holiday rates (such as Clerks — Private Sector or specific loaded classifications), casuals receive 275% of base rate! 4) Late Evening & Night Loadings (Mon–Fri): General Retail pays +25% loading after 6:00pm (125% perm / 150% casual); Fast Food pays +10% between 10:00pm and midnight, and +15% between midnight and 6:00am; Hospitality and Restaurant pay statutory flat-rate hourly allowances (~$2.95/hr late evening, ~$4.42/hr night). For a 15yo retail casual on a public holiday, 250% equals $31.28/hr; for an 18yo casual it is $48.68/hr; and for a 21yo adult casual it reaches a massive $69.53/hr.

17. Queensland Child Employment Act 2006 & Parent Consent Form CE1

In Queensland, employment of minors is strictly governed by the Child Employment Act 2006 and the Child Employment Regulation 2016. Under Section 10(3), an employer MUST NOT employ a school-aged child (a child under 16 who has not completed Year 10) until they have obtained a completed and signed Parent’s Consent Form CE1 (the chief-executive approved form; formerly Form 1). Key statutory requirements: 1) The form must state the exact hours the child is required to attend school, any other concurrent employment, and signed parental consent. 2) The employer must retain the signed Form CE1 on file at the workplace for inspection by industrial inspectors. 3) If the child’s school hours change (e.g. new term timetable or exam block), an updated Form CE1 must be completed and provided to the employer within 14 days. 4) Statutory hour caps: Max 12 hours per week during school terms, max 4 hours on any school day, max 8 hours on non-school days (weekends), and max 38 hours during school holidays (max 8 hrs/day). 5) Strict curfew: No work permitted between 10:00pm and 6:00am for workers under 16. Employers who breach child employment limits face statutory fines exceeding $15,000.

18. Under-15 Work Restrictions Across All 8 Australian States & Curfew Rules

Work laws for children under 15 vary by jurisdiction across Australia, but all share the statutory principle that schooling is compulsory and employment must be light work that does not harm health or education: 1) Victoria (*Child Employment Act 2003*): Employers MUST hold a Child Employment Licence from Workforce Inspectorate Victoria before hiring anyone under 15. Limits: max 3 hours/day on school days, max 12 hours/week in term, max 6 hours/day and 30 hours/week in holidays; strict 9:00pm–6:00am curfew; mandatory 30-min break after 3 hours. 2) New South Wales (*Industrial Relations (Child Employment) Act 2006*): Under-15s can work in retail and fast food outside school hours; max 4 hours on school days, max 1 shift/day, max 5 consecutive days, 9:00pm curfew on school nights; total school + work hours cannot exceed 50 hours in any week. 3) Queensland (*Child Employment Act 2006*): Mandatory Form CE1 parent consent; max 12 hrs/wk term, max 4 hrs/school day, max 38 hrs/holidays, 10:00pm curfew. 4) Western Australia (*Children and Community Services Act 2004*): 13–14yos require a signed Wageline Parental Permission Form before starting; work restricted between 6:00am and 10:00pm outside school times; fines up to $120k for non-compliance. 5) ACT (*Children and Young People Act 2008*): Children under 15 are restricted to max 10 hours/week of light work with mandatory parent consent. 6) SA, TAS, NT: Education legislation strictly prohibits employing school-aged children during school hours or scheduling shifts that cause fatigue or interfere with school progress (e.g. South Australia Education Act s 74 carries $10,000 fines).

19. General Retail Award Clause 11.3: The 1.5-Hour Student Shift Exception

While the standard casual engagement under the General Retail Industry Award 2020 [MA000004] is 3 hours, Clause 11.3 (and Clause 15.7 for part-time) creates a specific exception permitting a 1.5-hour minimum shift for secondary school students. To be lawful, all 4 criteria must be met: 1) The employee is a full-time secondary school student, 2) The shift is worked between 3:00pm and 6:30pm on a day the student is required to attend school, 3) The student agrees to work fewer than 3 hours, with the written approval of their parent or guardian, and 4) A longer shift is not possible due to either student availability or the operational needs of the employer. Critical Trap: The 6:30pm Hard Boundary. If an employer rosters a student past 6:30pm (e.g. 3:30pm to 7:00pm), or requires the student to stay even 5 minutes past 6:30pm, the 1.5-hour exception is completely VOID. In that case, the standard 3-hour minimum engagement applies and the employer MUST pay for the full 3 hours ($46.92+ for a 15yo casual)!

20. The Liquor Service Rule: Under-18s Earning 100% Adult Pay

One of the most valuable provisions in Australian workplace law protects young hospitality workers: Under the Restaurant Industry Award 2020 [MA000119] (Clause 18.4), Hospitality Industry (General) Award 2020 [MA000009] (Clause 18.4), and Registered and Licensed Clubs Award 2020 [MA000058] (Clause 18.4), junior percentage pay rates CANNOT be paid to any employee who serves or sells liquor. If a 16- or 17-year-old worker holds a state Responsible Service of Alcohol (RSA) certification and their job duties involve taking alcohol orders, pouring beer, mixing drinks, or carrying wine to tables (in jurisdictions where minors are legally permitted to serve alcohol under adult supervision), the employer is legally prohibited from applying the 50% or 60% junior rate! The employer MUST pay 100% of the adult Level 1 or Level 2 classification rate ($26.44–$27.81/hr base, or $33.05–$34.76/hr casual with 25% loading, plus applicable weekend penalty rates). Paying a junior rate while having a teen serve alcohol constitutes illegal wage theft under the Fair Work Act.

21. Word-for-Word Workplace Negotiation Scripts: Raises, Shift Swaps & Underpayment

Asserting your workplace rights is easier when you have clear, professional language: 1) Asking for a Pay Rise or Classification Bump after 6–12 months: "Hi [Manager Name], thank you for meeting with me. Over the past [9] months, I have taken on additional responsibilities including [training new team members / closing registers / stock inventory] while maintaining reliable attendance. Based on my proven contributions and modern award benchmarks, I would like to request a review of my classification to Level 2 / an increase in my hourly pay to $[X]/hr. What steps can we take to adjust my rate?" 2) Enforcing the 3-Hour Minimum Shift: "Hi [Manager Name], I understand the shop is quiet today. Just to confirm for payroll records, under the [Retail / Fast Food] Award, casual shifts have a statutory 3-hour minimum engagement. I will record the full 3 hours on my timesheet for today. Thank you for understanding!" 3) Setting Boundaries During High School Exam Weeks: "Hi [Manager Name], thank you for offering me the extra shifts next week. Because my Year [11/12] trial exams run from [Date] to [Date], I need to strictly limit my hours to my regular agreed shifts of [8] hours this week so I can focus on my studies. My schedule will be fully open for extra shifts as soon as exams finish on [Date]!" 4) Politely Addressing Payslip Underpayment: "Hi [Payroll / Manager], I was reviewing my latest payslip for the week ending [Date] and noticed my Sunday shift was paid at standard ordinary time rather than the [150% / 175%] award penalty rate. Could we please check the roster log together so the adjustment can be processed in the next pay cycle?"

22. Casual Conversion (s 66AAB Employee Choice) & The Fair Work Right to Disconnect (s 333M)

Two major statutory protections under the Fair Work Act 2009 empower casuals and young workers: 1) Casual Conversion via the "Employee Choice Pathway" (Section 66AAB): Under reforms introduced by the Closing Loopholes No. 2 Act, former Section 66B was replaced by an employee-initiated notification pathway under Section 66AAB. If you have been employed for at least 6 months (or 12 months with a small business employer having fewer than 15 employees) and believe you no longer meet the statutory definition of a casual employee (s 15A)—for example, you work a regular, ongoing pattern of hours with a mutual understanding of continuous work—you have a legally enforceable right to give written notice to convert to permanent part-time or full-time employment. Under Section 66AAC, your employer MUST consult with you and give a written response within 21 days, and can only refuse on specific, fair and reasonable operational grounds. Converting to permanent unlocks guaranteed weekly hours, 4 weeks paid annual leave, 10 days paid personal/sick leave, paid public holidays, and protection from unfair dismissal! 2) The Fair Work Right to Disconnect (Section 333M): Commencing August 2024 for non-small businesses and August 2025 for all small businesses, employees have a protected statutory workplace right to refuse to monitor, read, or respond to contact (calls, text messages, emails, or roster chats) from their employer outside rostered hours, unless the refusal is unreasonable (assessed under s 333M(3)). Under General Protections (s 340), employers cannot take adverse action (discipline you, cut your casual shifts, or dismiss you) for exercising this right, and the Fair Work Commission can issue binding stop orders under Section 333P.
