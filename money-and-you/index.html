Step 1 • Legal Prerequisite
ATO NAT 3092 / AusPost

Apply for Your Free Tax File Number (TFN)

Apply online through Australia Post or myGov using your 100-point ID (Birth Certificate [70pts] + School Student ID [40pts] = 110pts). It is 100% free (never pay a third-party fee!). Giving your TFN to your employer (via ATO Form NAT 3092) and bank stops 47% top marginal withholding tax.

Checkpoints: 0 of 5 done
Action Checkpoints:
Gather 100 points of ID (Australian Birth Certificate [70 pts] + School Photo ID [40 pts] = 110 pts)
Complete the online TFN application for Australian residents on ato.gov.au
Print or save the official application summary barcode
Book and attend your free 5-minute identity appointment at Australia Post
Store your 9-digit TFN securely once it arrives by mail within 28 days
Pro-Tip:

Never pay private third-party websites claiming to process a TFN for $50-$100. Applying through Australia Post and the ATO is completely free by law.

Statutory Basis: Taxation Administration Act 1953
Book a free identity appointment at Australia Post with your Birth Certificate (70pts) and Student Photo ID (40pts).

Step 3 • Healthcare Independence
Form MS004 & Form MS011

Claim Your Own Green Medicare Card

From age 15 in Australia, Services Australia allows you to get your own separate green Medicare card (via myGov or Form MS011 / MS004) to visit bulk-billing doctors independently with 100% confidential medical privacy. Parents are not notified.

Checkpoints: 0 of 5 done

Money & You Q&A Library

(18 topics)

Financial psychologist Dr. Brad Klontz identifies 4 core "Money Scripts" that shape financial behavior: Money Avoidance (believing money is bad or anxiety-inducing), Money Worship (believing money solves all problems), Money Status (equating self-worth with net worth and luxury brands), and Money Vigilance (alert, discreet, and focused on saving). Most financial choices are subconscious habits absorbed from parents and peers by age 7! Recognizing your dominant money script helps you spot self-sabotaging patterns—like spending to impress acquaintances or avoiding opening bank apps out of dread. Money is not a moral scorecard; it is simply a tool that provides autonomy, choices, and lifelong peace of mind.
