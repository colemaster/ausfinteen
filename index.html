1. What Are Modern Awards & How Do Junior Pay Rates Work?

Modern Awards are legally binding industry standards set by the Fair Work Commission. Entry-level junior pay is calculated as a percentage of the adult Level 1 base rate ($27.81/hr for 2026-27). For example, under the Fast Food Award [MA000003], workers under 16 receive 40% ($11.12/hr base, or $13.90/hr with 25% casual loading). Under the General Retail Award [MA000004], workers under 16 receive 45% ($12.51/hr base, or $15.64/hr casual). Paying below award rates is illegal wage theft.

2. What Is The 3-Hour Minimum Shift Rule Under Modern Awards?

Under major Australian awards including Fast Food [MA000003], Retail [MA000004], Pharmacy [MA000012], and Fitness [MA000094], casual employees are legally entitled to a minimum of 3 hours pay per shift. Under Restaurant [MA000119] and Hospitality [MA000009], the minimum is 2 hours. If your manager sends you home after 1 hour because the shop is quiet, they are still legally required to pay you for the full minimum shift duration!

3. What Are My Legal Rest Pause & Meal Break Entitlements?

Modern Awards mandate strict break schedules: 1) Shifts between 4 and 5 hours: 1 × 10-minute rest break (100% PAID by employer). 2) Shifts 5 to 7 hours: 1 × 10-minute paid rest break PLUS 1 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal break. 3) Shifts 7 to 10 hours: 2 × 10-minute paid rest breaks PLUS 1 × 30-to-60 minute unpaid meal break. Employers cannot force you to work through your paid rest pauses or deduct pay for 10-minute rest breaks.

4. What Are The State Working Hours & Curfew Rules For 15-Year-Olds?

Each state enforces statutory limits: In QLD (*Child Employment Act 2006*), 15yos who haven't finished Year 10 can work max 12 hrs/week in term, max 4 hrs on a school day, max 38 hrs in holidays, with a strict 10pm–6am curfew and a mandatory approved parent's consent form (the old "Form 1" number is deprecated). In VIC (*Child Employment Act 2003*), teens 15+ can work in retail/hospitality without an employer permit outside school hours. In WA, 15yos work under standard awards outside school hours. In SA, NSW, ACT, TAS, and NT, employment must not interfere with compulsory schooling.

5. How Do I Complete ATO Form NAT 3092 (TFN Declaration)?

When starting any new job, your employer must provide ATO Tax File Number Declaration Form (NAT 3092). Critical steps: 1) Fill in your legal name, date of birth, and 9-digit TFN. 2) At Question 8 ("Do you want to claim the tax-free threshold from this payer?"), tick YES if this is your primary or only job. This stops income tax from being withheld on earnings up to $18,200/year.

6. Are Unpaid Work Trials Legal In Australia?

Under Fair Work Ombudsman guidelines, unpaid trials are only lawful if they are brief skills tests strictly necessary to demonstrate relevant capabilities (e.g. demonstrating how to make 2 coffees or operate a till for 1–2 hours under direct supervision). Any trial that involves general shift labor, lasts multiple hours, or occurs during commercial service MUST be paid at the full award hourly rate.

7. What Is The 100-Point ID Checklist For 15-Year-Olds?

To open bank accounts, obtain a TFN, or pass employer HR onboarding, 15-year-olds need 100 points of identification: 1) Primary ID (70 pts): Australian Birth Certificate or Australian Passport. 2) Secondary Photo ID (40 pts): Current High School Student ID Card (with photo) or Learner Driver Licence. 3) Supporting ID (25 pts): Green Medicare Card, Bank Debit Card, or an official letter from your high school principal.

8. How Do I Fill Out ATO Super Choice Form NAT 13080?

Under Super Stapling legislation, your superannuation fund follows you from job to job. Complete ATO Form NAT 13080 (Superannuation Standard Choice Form) to ensure your employer pays super into your chosen fund. Provide your Fund Name, Unique Superannuation Identifier (USI), and Member Number. This prevents duplicate accounts and stops multiple monthly admin fee deductions.

9. What Are The 10 Mandatory Items On A Legal Australian Payslip?

Under Section 536 of the Fair Work Act 2009, employers must provide a payslip within 1 working day of payday. Mandatory details include: 1) Employer name & ABN, 2) Employee name, 3) Pay period dates, 4) Gross and net pay, 5) Hourly pay rate and hours worked, 6) Any penalty rates or loadings (+25% casual), 7) Deductions (PAYG tax), 8) Superannuation contribution amount and fund name, 9) Leave balances (for perm staff), and 10) Employment classification.

10. Young Worker Safety, Workplace Health & WorkCover Rights

Every young Australian worker is protected under SafeWork legislation. Employers have a non-delegable duty of care to provide safe working conditions, personal protective equipment (PPE), and proper machine training. You have the legal right to refuse unsafe work without fear of dismissal. If you are injured on shift, report it immediately to your supervisor and consult a doctor—all medical expenses and lost wages are covered by statutory state WorkCover insurance.

11. Workplace Scripts: How To Hand In Resumes & Call In Sick Professionally

Professional workplace communication builds reputation: 1) Handing in a resume in person: Visit cafes/shops during quiet hours (2:00pm–4:00pm Tuesday–Thursday), dress neatly, ask for the hiring manager, and deliver a concise pitch. 2) Calling in sick: Call your manager at least 2 hours before your shift begins; state clearly that you are unwell and offer a doctor's medical certificate or statutory declaration.

12. School-Based Apprenticeships & Traineeships (SATs)

School-Based Apprenticeships and Traineeships (SATs) allow high school students in Years 10, 11, and 12 to combine paid work (typically 1 day/week earning $15–$22/hr), structured vocational training at TAFE, and regular school studies. SATs count directly toward your Queensland Certificate of Education (QCE) or state senior certificate, letting you graduate Year 12 with a nationally recognized qualification and zero student debt.

13. Cleaning Award Secret: Full Adult Pay From Day One (No Junior Rates!)

The Cleaning Services Award (MA000022) has NO junior percentage rates — a 15-year-old cleaner earns the full adult Level 1 rate of $27.08/hr ($33.85/hr casual with 25% loading) from 1 July 2026, plus Saturday 150%, Sunday 200% and public holiday 250%. That can double what Retail juniors earn at the same age. Exception: trolley collectors hired via contractors (not directly by supermarkets) DO get junior rates — always confirm who your legal employer is.

14. Retail vs Fast Food: Which Pays More on Sundays & Public Holidays?

Both pay a $27.81/hr adult base ($34.76 casual) from 1 July 2026, but penalties differ. Retail casuals earn Sunday 175% ($48.67/hr) and public holidays 250% ($69.52/hr). Fast Food Level 1 casuals earn Sunday 150% ($41.71/hr) and public holidays 250%. A Sunday + public-holiday-heavy Retail roster can beat a Fast Food base rate by $7+/hr — compare rosters, not just base rates, and screenshot PACT rates to check every payslip.
